## 1. Purpose

This specification defines the controlling MVP permission model for collaboration inside a CoBudget budget space. It defines role membership, action authority, resource visibility, ownership safeguards, cross-budget isolation, authorization inputs, audit evidence, and denial behavior.

The model is designed to be complete enough for interface design, server-side policy design, data modeling, threat review, and deterministic authorization testing. It does not grant legal, fiduciary, or bank-account authority.

## 2. Scope and vocabulary

### 2.1 Membership model

* A role is a property of a person's membership in one budget space, not a global account role.
* A person has exactly one active role per budget space for MVP.
* Membership and Viewer visibility profiles in one budget space confer no authority or visibility in another.
* A budget space has exactly one active **Primary Owner** and may have multiple active **Co-owners**.
* Pending, rejected, expired, revoked, or inactive memberships confer no access, actions, derived data, exports, searches, or alerts.
* A Viewer visibility profile narrows or expands only readable scope; it never elevates the Viewer role or grants mutation authority.

### 2.2 Roles

| Role | MVP meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Primary Owner | Required controlling owner. Has complete budget-space administration subject to connection-specific authority and protected-action safeguards. |
| Co-owner | Optional second owner with day-to-day administration. Cannot remove or demote the Primary Owner, transfer Primary ownership, or delete the budget space. |
| Collaborator | Equal financial contributor and full-budget planning participant, but not a budget-space administrator. May perform approved planning and reconciliation mutations, export full financial data, and authorize/manage only bank connections they personally authorize. Cannot administer ownership, membership, permissions, another person's bank connection, customer administrative-history exports, shared settings, or budget-space lifecycle. |
| Viewer | Default-deny read-only role assigned exactly one owner-managed visibility profile: Full budget, Planning, Category group, or Account group. Profile scope inherits safe descendants and interpretation metadata; it never grants mutation or bulk-export authority. |
| Accountability Partner | Accepted formal support role that is financially read-only across comprehensive budget-space financial and schedule visibility within its role boundary, subject to masking, consent, and privacy controls. It may create personal firm-alert acknowledgements and attributed comments on supported readable targets; those interactions never mutate financial, schedule, permission, membership, or configuration state. It is not a broad Viewer grant. |

### 2.3 Planning entities

| Entity | MVP meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Budget space | The long-lived collaborative container for memberships, financial records, plans, schedules, and retained history. Budget-space deletion is a separate Primary-only lifecycle action. |
| Budget schedule | The rule or confirmed structure that defines budget-period boundaries. |
| Budget period | One concrete instance produced by a budget schedule. |
| Category | A long-lived financial classification that may be used across periods. A confirmed category is never hard-deleted in MVP. |
| Category target | The planned amount for one category in one budget period. |
| Budget plan | The set of category targets for one draft or future budget period. Corrections to a plan that became active or historical are retained as versions rather than destructive replacement. |

### 2.4 Permission notation

| Code | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Allow | The role may act when all resource, membership, connection, and state predicates pass. |
| Read | Read-only access subject to the role's visibility boundary. |
| Scoped | Available only within the Viewer membership's current visibility profile and its inherited safe scope. |
| Authorizer | Available only to the person who authorized the specific bank connection. |
| Own | Available only when the active member is the original author of the interaction record and still has access to its target. |
| Primary | Primary Owner only, with the protected-action safeguards stated in this specification. |
| Deny | Not permitted. The control is not offered; a direct attempt is denied, changes no protected state, and is audited. |

## 5. Resource-scope and masking rules

### 5.1 Viewer visibility hierarchy

Viewer authorization is hierarchical rather than an independent grant per item:

`budget-space membership → Viewer role → one visibility profile → zero or more same-type visibility groups → inherited resources and safe interpretation envelope`

1. A new Viewer membership starts with no profile and therefore no financial, schedule, search, report, derived, or alert visibility.
2. An owner assigns exactly one profile type at a time. Account-group and Category-group scopes cannot be combined on one Viewer membership; selecting multiple groups within one profile uses union semantics.
3. **Full budget:** includes all shared financial and schedule resources within the Viewer field boundary. It excludes administration, bank-connection management/configuration, Viewer-initiated bulk export, personal settings/state, and security-only audit data. An owner-authorized snapshot remains a separate sharing action under §5.8.
4. **Planning:** includes budget plans, category labels/groups, targets, bills, goals, and schedules. It excludes accounts, balances, transactions, actual-spending/variance indicators, reconciliation data, and any derived value that depends on those actuals.
5. **Category group:** includes one or more owner-managed named category visibility groups and their descendant categories, targets, related bills/goals, category activity, relevant schedule/history, and readable interactions. It excludes unrelated categories, account balances, connection context, global totals, and source-account identity except a non-identifying label strictly required to understand visible activity.
6. **Account group:** includes one or more owner-managed named account visibility groups, each selected account's safe identity, balance, all readable transactions, reconciliation history, and readable interactions. Transaction category labels may appear as interpretation metadata, but whole-category targets, unrelated category activity, and global budget totals do not.
7. A visibility group is a reusable owner-managed scope root, such as “Household accounts” or “Housing categories.” Assigning resources to, moving them between, or removing them from a group when the change affects any Viewer is owner-only sharing administration. Collaborator financial-resource authority does not confer this permission.
8. Every profile automatically includes a fixed **interpretation envelope**: names, period labels, category labels, safe status, masked source label, and actor identity/role only when needed to understand accessible content. It never includes hidden amounts, balances, counts, totals, relationships, or existence signals. Envelope fields are not separately configurable.
9. For a transaction split between visible and hidden categories, Category-group scope exposes only a synthetic category-activity record containing the visible allocation and safe merchant/date context. It does not expose the original transaction total, account balance/identity, hidden allocation count, or the existence of another category allocation.
10. Search, history, firm alerts, totals, trends, and reports inherit the profile. Derived output is produced only from complete authorized inputs and is labeled “Shared view—not the full budget” unless the profile is Full budget. If a report requires unavailable inputs, the report is unavailable rather than silently partial. A Viewer never receives informational alerts.
11. A Viewer cannot edit, comment, initiate bulk export, connect, acknowledge informational alerts, or administer a shared resource. A Viewer may only download a currently authorized owner-created snapshot under §5.8. Profile changes are atomic and immediately invalidate old sessions, links, caches, jobs, reports, alerts, and pending packages.

### 5.2 Derived-data noninterference

Authorization filtering occurs before aggregation, search indexing, report calculation, notification eligibility, cache selection, and export generation. If a derived value would reveal the existence, count, value, timing, category, relationship, or trend of an unreadable resource, the value is omitted or replaced with a non-revealing permission explanation. It is never represented as zero.

### 5.3 Accountability Partner boundary

An active Accountability Partner receives comprehensive financial and schedule context required for the formal support role and is financially read-only, including for supported history, reports, firm alerts, and informational alerts. The role excludes credentials, private security configuration, bank-connection management, unrestricted export, ownership or membership administration, and all financial or schedule mutation. It may create personal firm-alert acknowledgements and attributed comments on supported readable targets; those interactions create attributed records only and confer no financial or administrative authority.

“Comprehensive” means all budget-space financial and schedule resources, not all stored fields. The fixed visible field boundary includes all budget plans, categories, bills, goals, shared accounts, balances, transaction history, transaction amount/date/category/status/merchant-or-payee display name, relevant shared notes, schedules, reconciliation history, reports, totals, trends, alerts, and shared comments. It excludes credentials/tokens/authentication data, unmasked full account or card numbers, connection-management details, another person's notification settings or personal alert/acknowledgement state, invitation/grant/ownership administration, customer administrative-history export data, internal provider payloads, privileged security/fraud/support telemetry or notes, and cross-space data. The role has no resource-level grants; use Viewer for partial sharing.

### 5.4 Personal notification settings

Notification channels, delivery preferences, alert instances, and acknowledgement state belong to the recipient's personal account, not to a budget space and not to another member. Each person independently configures supported email, push, and SMS channels and preferences by supported event or category. Other members cannot view or change those destinations or preferences and cannot acknowledge, clear, or hide another recipient's alert.

Eligibility continues to follow the accepted role, current authorization, masking, and supported-event rules; actual external delivery follows the recipient's personal channel and delivery preferences. No budget-space member can view or alter another member's notification settings. CBD-69, CBD-70, CBD-71, and CBD-72 now use this same boundary.

MVP alert event types, trigger meanings, and numeric thresholds are built in and cannot be changed by a member. System-owned deduplication and cooldown behavior prevents repeat noise without becoming a shared setting. Each recipient may configure only supported external channels, event/category delivery preferences, quiet hours, time zone, and digest/immediate behavior. There is no recipient-configurable privacy or verbosity choice: external notification content is fixed by purpose tier under `CBD-12-AC21` and the CBD-92 transport ceilings, and no preference, setting, or opt-in widens it. Protected detail exists only in authenticated in-app views. An eligible in-app instance is always created. The recipient may archive or dismiss only their own instance from the active view; this changes neither the underlying alert fact nor eligibility nor another recipient's instance. Custom thresholds and arbitrary alert rules are highest-priority post-MVP work under FF-009.

#### 5.4.1 Three-record alert model

Every supported alert is represented through three distinct correlated record types:

| Layer | Scope and required state | Owns | Must not own |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Shared alert event/fact | Budget-space scoped. Stores event ID, built-in type, firm/informational classification, source resource and material revision, safe fact payload, trigger/observed time, deduplication key, lifecycle, and audit linkage. | Trigger evaluation, source/material-revision deduplication, system cooldown, informational resolution, correlation to instances. | Recipient acknowledgement/read/archive state, personal destinations/preferences, channel attempt status. |
| Recipient-personal in-app instance | Personal-account and membership scoped, unique per shared event and eligible recipient. Stores instance ID, event ID, recipient/membership, authorization version, created/closed time, read state, firm acknowledgement, and personal archive/dismiss state. | Mandatory eligible in-app presentation and recipient-only interaction state. | Shared financial truth, another recipient's state, external delivery outcome. |
| Delivery attempt | Personal-account, instance, and channel scoped. Stores attempt ID, instance/event correlation, channel, privacy-safe destination reference, rendered-template version/hash, requested/sent/failed/suppressed status and time, provider receipt/error class, and retry lineage. | Optional external delivery, quiet hours/digest scheduling, retries, provider outcome, operational audit. | In-app-instance creation, shared event lifecycle, acknowledgement, raw destination exposure to other members. |

1. The system creates at most one shared event for one built-in rule, source identity, and material source revision. Event-level cooldown/deduplication suppresses repeated equivalent facts; a materially changed source revision may create a new linked event. Delivery retries never create another event.
2. Eligibility is evaluated at event creation, material revision, and relevant membership/role/profile/visibility change while the event is active. Instance fan-out is idempotent on event plus recipient. Newly eligible recipients receive an instance for a still-active event; loss of eligibility closes access to that recipient's instance and suppresses unsent delivery without affecting others.
3. Every eligible recipient receives one mandatory in-app instance regardless of external-channel preferences or delivery success. A failed/retried email, push, or SMS attempt never duplicates the in-app instance.
4. A recipient may read, acknowledge a firm instance, or archive/dismiss their own instance only. Acknowledgement and archive/dismiss are separate personal states and never mutate the shared event, other instances, or financial state.
5. When an informational source resolves, the shared event becomes resolved, all associated open instances close/self-clear, and queued unsent attempts are suppressed. Informational instances never have acknowledgement state.
6. A firm event records a settled fact that occurred and does not self-clear. Later correction or material revision is linked as a new event/revision; each recipient's existing instance remains personal until that recipient acknowledges or archives/dismisses it, subject to current authorization.
7. External delivery is attempted only after instance creation and a send-time authorization/privacy check. Personal channel preferences, quiet hours, digest selection, and redacted destination references are evaluated only at the delivery layer. Other budget-space members cannot inspect them.
8. Retention follows layer purpose: shared events follow the underlying financial/audit retention; recipient instances follow personal-account and membership retention; delivery attempts retain only the minimum operational/security evidence for the applicable delivery-retention period. Expiring one layer never deletes the underlying financial fact or another recipient's state.
9. Audit events carry correlation IDs but preserve privacy boundaries: shared-event audit records trigger/dedup/resolution; instance audit records eligibility and personal state changes; delivery audit records channel outcome without raw secrets or destinations.

### 5.5 Destructive financial-resource contract

1. Primary Owners, Co-owners, and Collaborators have the same approved individual-resource authority for bills, goals, categories, and clearly labeled manual transactions. Viewer remains read-only. Accountability Partner remains financially read-only while retaining only the separately permitted personal acknowledgements and attributed comments. **Proposed (September 15, 2026), not yet approved:** this same shared authority extends to a manual account (row 36); pending approval, a manual account is governed by row 36 and this note rather than by an approved inclusion in this list.
2. A never-confirmed, never-active, unreferenced bill or goal draft may be permanently discarded. A confirmed bill or goal is archived/restored and never hard-deleted: archival preserves its history, removes it from active planning, and stops new future occurrences or targets.
3. A manual transaction may be removed from the customer-visible financial record. Removal requires current authorization and explicit confirmation that identifies the transaction and summarizes changes to totals, reports, and alerts.
4. Manual-transaction removal is blocked while the record participates in an active reconciliation, match, split, schedule occurrence, bill fulfillment, goal contribution, or other financial dependency. The actor must first use the supported unmatch, unlink, or correction workflow; removal never silently breaks a relationship.
5. On removal, the manual transaction and its child interaction records leave ordinary views, search, reports, new exports, and calculations. Totals, category usage, forecasts, and eligible alerts are recalculated atomically. Restore reverses those effects against current authorization and current resource state.
6. An authorized Primary Owner, Co-owner, or Collaborator may restore the transaction for 30 calendar days after removal. At the end of that window, the financial payload and retained child content may be purged according to the applicable retention policy.
7. A minimal non-financial audit tombstone remains after purge: internal record identifier, manual-origin marker, creator identifier, removal actor, removal time, purge time, budget-space identifier, and audit-chain reference. It contains no amount, merchant/payee, category, note/comment body, or other removed financial content.
8. Every archive, restore, draft discard, manual removal, manual restoration, blocked dependency check, and purge records its actor or system cause, target, time, prior/new lifecycle state, authorization version, outcome, and recalculation result. Concurrent or stale actions fail without partial state change.

### 5.6 Notes and comments lifecycle

1. A supported shared note/comment is an attributed interaction record attached to one financial or schedule resource. It cannot change transaction values, categories, schedules, reconciliation relationships, or other financial state.
2. Create, read, edit, and remove authorization is evaluated against the target resource at request time. If the actor cannot currently read the target, the interaction and its existence are not disclosed.
3. Primary Owners, Co-owners, Collaborators, and Accountability Partners may create comments on readable supported targets. Viewer commenting is not part of MVP.
4. Only the original author may edit or remove their interaction, and only while their membership is active and the target remains readable. No owner or other member may edit, remove, hide, or moderate another author's contribution.
5. An edit displays an “edited” indicator. Audit retains actor, interaction/target identifiers, timestamps, action, and integrity evidence; it does not expose prior or removed bodies through ordinary history.
6. Removal makes the body unavailable in ordinary reads, searches, reports, notifications, and exports. When replies or necessary thread context exist, show a neutral “Comment removed” tombstone without the body; otherwise the interaction may disappear from the ordinary thread. Minimal audit metadata and reply linkage remain.
7. If membership or target access is later lost, previously authored content remains attributed and follows the target's visibility, but the former author cannot read, edit, or remove it through that lost membership. A target's archival or recoverable removal also hides its interactions; restoration restores visibility under current authorization.
8. Current, non-removed interaction content may appear only in a report or export that includes its currently readable target. Removed bodies and content from unreadable targets are never included.
9. File attachments and `@mentions` are excluded from MVP. Serious abuse, unlawful content, or accidental disclosure is handled only by a separate platform-level safety/support process, not by any budget-space role permission.

### 5.7 Customer administrative-history export

1. Only the current Primary Owner may request this package. Generation and download each require current Primary authority; generation additionally requires fresh reauthentication and an explicit irreversible-download warning.
2. The versioned allowlist contains only budget-space identity; safe event identifier/type/time; safe actor and affected-member display identifiers and roles; masked invitation channel; invitation/membership status transitions; role changes; Viewer profile, scope-group, and authorization-version changes; Primary/Co-owner appointment, removal, and transfer outcomes; protected-action type, safe result category, and correlation reference.
3. Redaction occurs before serialization. Exclude full email addresses and phone numbers except the requester's own verified contact, authentication/session/device/network evidence, IP addresses, credentials/tokens, bank-connection events or configuration, personal notification/alert state, financial records, free-form internal reasons, staff identities, provider payloads, internal object payloads, security detection logic, abuse/fraud/risk scores or signals, privileged telemetry, and support notes.
4. Denied or failed protected actions may appear only as customer-safe action type, time, actor/target role context, result category, and correlation reference. Internal denial evidence and security rationale never appear.
5. The encrypted package is bound to the requesting Primary Owner, cannot be reassigned, and expires permanently no later than 24 hours after generation. Authorization is rechecked at download; ownership or membership loss denies download. Expiry removes the server-side package. Source administrative records follow their separately approved retention policy and are not extended by export generation.
6. Package generation, download, expiry, denial, and deletion are audited without copying package contents or privileged excluded fields into the export audit event.

### 5.8 Owner-authorized Viewer snapshot

1. Only a current Primary Owner or Co-owner may initiate generation for one specifically identified active Viewer. The action is an explicit owner-authorized disclosure, not Viewer self-service or role-granted bulk-export authority. A Collaborator, Viewer, or Accountability Partner cannot initiate, regenerate, expand, redirect, or reuse a package.
2. Generation uses the recipient Viewer's current membership, role, profile, scope groups, budget-space identifier, and authorization versions. Generation and download each recheck those inputs. Any relevant membership, role, profile, group, scope, or authorization-version change invalidates the package immediately.
3. Full-budget profile may include current Viewer-readable financial, planning, schedule, reconciliation, ordinary-history, safe account-identity, and current shared-comment data. Planning profile may include plans, targets, category structure, bills, goals, and schedules but no accounts, transactions, balances, reconciliation, actual-spending/variance indicators, or actual-derived values.
4. Category-group profile may include selected category groups, their authorized descendants, and synthetic visible-allocation activity. It excludes account identity/balance, original split totals, hidden-allocation count or values, hidden categories, and any signal from hidden inputs. Account-group profile may include selected safely masked accounts, balances, complete readable transactions, and reconciliation history but excludes whole-budget/category targets, global totals, and unrelated accounts.
5. A report or derived value is included only when the current profile supports it and every required input is readable. Unsupported or incomplete reports are omitted rather than rendered as misleading partial outputs. Current non-removed comments may appear only with their currently readable target.
6. Every profile excludes credentials, tokens, authentication material, private connection configuration, hidden or cross-space resources, values derived from hidden inputs, another member's personal alert/acknowledgement/delivery state, membership/ownership/permission administration, customer administrative-history data, provider payloads, and privileged security, fraud, risk, telemetry, staff, or support information.
7. Before generation, the owner sees and explicitly confirms the intended Viewer, profile, scope groups, safe content summary, expiration, and irreversible-download consequence. The encrypted package is bound to that Viewer, cannot be reassigned or exposed through a public link, is non-renewable, and expires permanently no later than 24 hours after generation.
8. Generation, download, denial, invalidation, expiry, and server-side deletion are rate-limited and audited with owner, recipient, budget space, profile/scope and authorization versions, package identifier, time, outcome, and safe reason code. Audit payloads do not copy package contents or excluded data.
9. The physical file format and field names may be finalized in an implementation design, but that work may only narrow or encode this approved semantic allowlist; it cannot broaden the package or reopen the permission decision without explicit change control.

## 6. Ownership, transfer, recovery, and deletion

### 6.1 Protected-action contract

Co-owner removal, Primary ownership transfer, and budget-space deletion require a fresh reauthentication result bound to the actor, action, budget space, and short validity window plus an explicit confirmation that names the consequence. Authorization and current state are rechecked at commit time.

### 6.2 Primary ownership transfer

The proposed MVP transfer is an atomic transaction:

1. The current Primary Owner selects any other active Co-owner, Collaborator, Viewer, or Accountability Partner. Pending, inactive, revoked, expired, and invited-but-not-active people are ineligible.
2. The system shows the recipient a versioned disclosure of the full financial and administrative access, protected Primary powers, outgoing-role consequences, and unchanged connection-authorizer boundaries.
3. The recipient authenticates and explicitly accepts becoming Primary Owner. Acceptance from a Viewer or Accountability Partner directly authorizes the role expansion; no intermediate role change is required.
4. The current Primary Owner freshly reauthenticates and explicitly confirms the same transition and consequences.
5. At commit, the system rechecks both authenticated sessions, actor authority, recipient active membership and role, membership/authorization versions, and the one-Primary invariant.
6. The recipient becomes the sole Primary Owner. The former Primary Owner always becomes a Co-owner, regardless of how many Co-owners already exist; existing Co-owners are unchanged.
7. If the recipient was a Viewer, the Viewer profile and scope groups end. If the recipient was an Accountability Partner, the Partner role state and role-specific alert eligibility end. Required consent and audit history remains retained; personal notification preferences remain personal-account state. All stale views, reports, jobs, packages, links, and alert eligibility are recalculated or invalidated under the new role.
8. Both parties and affected current owners receive a safe notice, and the complete transition is audited as one correlated event group. No bank-connection authority transfers.

### 6.3 Recovery and orphan prevention

* Ordinary support cannot silently assign or replace a Primary Owner.
* A budget space cannot deactivate its last Primary Owner, complete deletion of that person's account, or accept a membership mutation that would leave no active Primary Owner.
* Every member may revoke their own participation without another party's approval, with one bounded exception: the sole active Primary Owner cannot self-revoke, because departure would leave the budget space with no owner. This is a limit on abandoning a budget space, not a limit on ending involvement. The Primary Owner retains two unilateral exits that require no other party's approval — transfer Primary ownership to an accepted recipient (§6.2), or archive the budget space (§6.5). A Primary Owner who holds ownership of more than one budget space is unaffected in the others. The interface must present both exits whenever a sole Primary Owner attempts to leave, and must never leave them without a supported way out.
* When a sole Primary Owner becomes permanently unavailable, the budget space is recovered by **archival, never by takeover**. After **90 consecutive days with no authenticated Primary Owner activity** in the budget space, any active Co-owner or Collaborator may request archival. The request creates the mandatory authenticated in-app notice for every active member and for the Primary Owner and opens a **14-day objection window**. An external copy of a notice about the Primary Owner routes only to that owner's verified private safety channel where one is configured; if none is configured, it is unavailable, or delivery fails, the in-app notice is retained and surfaced and no other external destination is used as a fallback. Other members' own notices follow their ordinary personal delivery preferences within the approved channel ceilings. (RI-93-012, approved August 16, 2026; focused amendment authorized under FU-95-020.) Any authenticated Primary Owner activity, or an objection from the Primary Owner, cancels the request outright. If the window closes uncontested, the space archives under §6.5.
* This path grants no new authority to anyone. It cannot transfer ownership, change a role, widen a Viewer profile, reach a bank connection, or unlock an export. Its only effect is to move the space from live to archived, so members are not left with contributed financial data frozen in a space nobody can act on. The requester, every notification, every objection, the cancellation or completion, and the resulting archival are all audited.
* Support-mediated transfer of Primary ownership to any party, member or not, remains blocked. It requires a separately specified, identity-verified recovery procedure and security review, and until that procedure is approved such requests are refused rather than resolved through an administrative shortcut. The 90-day and 14-day values above are MVP product parameters and may be tuned without reopening this decision.
* If any connection authorizer loses active membership, new synchronization through that connection stops immediately; previously imported financial records and provenance remain; private connection configuration becomes inaccessible; later rejoining requires reauthorization; authority never transfers automatically.
* When that authorizer is permanently gone, the connection becomes a **permanently read-only orphaned connection**. It never synchronizes again, no member may adopt or reauthorize it, and its private configuration stays inaccessible to everyone including the Primary Owner. Its imported records and provenance remain readable so historical totals and reconciliation stay intact and explainable, and the interface labels it as orphaned with the reason. Coverage resumes only when an entitled member authorizes a **new** connection to the same logical account under permission 31; the two are then associated under PM-72-011 and overlapping imports deduplicate. Nobody ever inherits another person's provider authorization.
* A joint external account may be represented by multiple independent connections, one per consenting authorizer. CoBudget may associate them with one logical account only through a reliable provider identity or explicit confirmation, must deduplicate overlapping imports, and must preserve each connection's separate consent, management, provenance, and revocation boundary.

### 6.4 Budget-space deletion

Deletion is Primary-only and must not be conflated with leaving a membership.

**Deletion routes through archival.** A budget space is never purged directly from the live state. The Primary Owner archives it under §6.5, then requests deletion of the archived space. This keeps one lifecycle rather than two and gives every destructive step the same shape as the manual-transaction contract already approved in §5.5.

1. Requesting deletion of an archived budget space is a Primary-only protected action requiring fresh reauthentication and an explicit confirmation naming the budget space and stating that the financial payload will be permanently destroyed.
2. The request opens a **30-calendar-day restore window**. Throughout it the space stays archived and fully readable under §6.5, and the Primary Owner may restore it to archived-without-pending-deletion at any time. Every active member is notified when the window opens, when it is cancelled, and shortly before it closes. The §6.5 item 11 export path stays open for the whole window, so notice that the payload is about to be purged is never notice without a remedy. Members are told twice that their contributed records will be destroyed; they must be able to keep a copy of what they may already read before that happens.
3. At the end of the window the financial payload, planning history, reconciliation history, interactions, and imported records are permanently purged. Purge is irreversible.
4. A **minimal non-financial tombstone** remains: internal budget-space identifier, creation and archival times, deleting actor, purge time, and audit-chain linkage. It contains no amount, balance, merchant or payee, category, account identity, member roster, comment body, or other removed content.
5. Bank connections stop and are removed at purge. Each connection's individual authorizer retains their separate right to revoke provider authorization directly with the institution; purge never revokes on their behalf.
6. Every request, notification, cancellation, restoration, purge, and denial is audited, and audit-chain linkage survives the purge.

### 6.5 Budget-space archival

Archival is the Primary Owner's supported alternative to an exit that would orphan the budget space. It is a distinct lifecycle action from both deletion (§6.4) and membership revocation.

1. Only the current Primary Owner may archive a budget space, after fresh reauthentication and an explicit confirmation that names the budget space and states the consequences for every active member.
2. Archival preserves data. Financial records, planning history, reconciliation history, interactions, and audit evidence remain intact and readable as history. Archival erases nothing and is not a soft delete.
3. Archival ends active use for every member: no financial or planning mutations, no invitations or role changes, no new manual transactions or comments, no bank-connection synchronization, and no new alert generation. Existing recipient-personal alert instances follow their ordinary personal retention.
4. Every active member is notified. Members retain read access consistent with their role and, for a Viewer, their current visibility profile at the time of archival, subject to the access mechanics decided under OD-72-05.
5. Bank connections stop synchronizing. Each connection's individual authorizer keeps their separate right to disconnect and revoke provider authorization; archival never transfers connection authority.
6. Archival does not remove the Primary Owner. The one-Primary invariant (PM-72-008) continues to hold for an archived budget space, so the space is never orphaned.
7. Commit is atomic against current authority and lifecycle state, and the outcome is fully audited. A denied or stale attempt changes no state.
8. **Archival has no countdown.** An archived budget space is retained indefinitely and nothing is erased by archival itself. Only an explicit Primary-only deletion request under §6.4 starts a retention clock.
9. **Restoration is Primary-only**, requires fresh reauthentication and explicit confirmation, and returns the space to its live state with every member's role and Viewer profile as they stood at archival. Restoration re-enables mutations and alert generation but never re-enables a stopped bank connection, which requires fresh authorization by an entitled member.
10. **Member access after archival is read-only at the scope each member held at the moment of archival**, including a Viewer's profile and scope groups as they then stood. No role or profile may be changed while archived, so archival can neither widen nor narrow anyone's visibility. Accountability Partner masking, cross-space isolation, and every §5.2 derived-data rule continue to apply.
11. **Export follows read scope.** A member who held export or snapshot authority under permissions 20a, 20b, or 21 at the moment of archival retains it against that frozen archival-time scope. Archival preserves each member's route to a portable copy of what they may already read; it never becomes a way to strip that route. This grants no authority a member did not hold at archival, and it discloses nothing beyond the frozen scope: a Collaborator exports their archival-time readable financial data, only the Primary Owner exports administrative history, and a Viewer still cannot self-serve — an owner may create a §5.8 snapshot bounded by that Viewer's frozen profile, which remains the Viewer's only path to a copy. Every live control continues to apply: the versioned allowlist, fresh reauthentication, authorization at generation and download, recipient binding, encryption, rate limits, permanent package expiry within 24 hours, and complete audit. Because the archival-time scope is immutable, the invalidate-on-change trigger in §5.7 and §5.8 cannot fire and is not needed; expiry, recipient binding, and the download recheck remain the operative controls, and the frozen scope removes the stale-scope risk those rules exist to catch.
12. A budget space archived through the §6.3 inactive-owner path behaves identically. It retains its Primary Owner, and only that Primary Owner may restore or delete it.
