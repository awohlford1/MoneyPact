## 1. Purpose and contract

This inventory is the versioned catalog of every customer-facing message in the invitation, consent, membership-change, revocation, removal, and ownership-transfer lifecycle. Each row defines the message's audience, trigger, required semantic content, and prohibited content.

This inventory defines **semantics, not strings**. Exact copy, notification templates, template hashes, localization, accessibility evidence, and specialist review remain gated under `FU-95-017` and `EG-91-006`/CBD-106, and no message ships until the Product Owner approves its exact strings. The inventory version participates in consent versioning: the consent-copy version recorded in consent evidence resolves to the exact approved version of the disclosure messages defined here.

## 2. Cross-cutting semantic rules

Every message in §4 obeys all of the following, stated once here and not repeated per row:

1. **Approved `RI-93-016` standard.** Recorded consent or reauthentication is never presented as proof of voluntariness. Channel control is never presented as sole control. Delivered or recipient-controlled copies are never described as remotely revocable or erasable. No message characterizes another person's circumstances or reasons. No message implies authority or accountability the product does not support. Irreversible consequences appear before the action, never only after.
2. **Voluntary, accessible, nonjudgmental.** Consent, decline, and revocation copy is voluntary in tone, meets the inherited SD-071-048 accessibility requirements, avoids blame or judgment, and always includes a safe path to decline or leave (CBD-73-AC13).
3. **Channel ceilings.** Push, SMS, and routine email remain content-free of protected detail; protected detail appears only on authenticated in-app surfaces; purpose-specific email content follows its approved allowlist.
4. **Non-enumeration.** No message, status, timing difference, count, pagination shape, audit projection, export row, or error reveals account existence, a block, a pair limit, verification progress, or another person's private state.
5. **Unaccepted-request projection is presentation-equivalent.** A block- or limit-suppressed request and a real request carrying restricted provider-failure metadata follow the same customer-visible acknowledgement, masked target, safe status chronology, resend/cancel controls, authoritative expiry and normalized terminal schedule, response class, and latency envelope as an ordinary Pending request. A bounce or rejection may set the restricted active `Failed` delivery state, but it does not terminalize invitation authority, invalidate the code, alter ceremony/acceptance eligibility, or advance customer chronology. The holder follows the same resolution, verification, attachment, disclosure, and acceptance path as any ordinary Pending invitation. Customer surfaces never claim provider delivery/failure or expose block, limit, or synthetic classification. Restricted security evidence may retain the provider result; customer status, audit, and export may not.
6. **Canonical ceremony order.** A link holder sees only the generic entry surface and then proves the exact invited channel. After that proof, the verified holder may decline this invitation once without authenticating an account. Authentication or account creation attached to the account that will hold the membership is required before protected disclosure, destination nomination, consent, acceptance, or creation of a persistent account-bound inviter block. Ambiguity, abandonment, or interruption creates neither a decline nor a block. Proof from one invitation, ceremony, account, or destination is never reusable in another.
7. **No pre-consent data.** No surface before acceptance shows actual financial data, hidden resources, private member details, or an unauthorized member list (`RI-93-009`).
8. **Mandatory lifecycle notice survives membership end.** Revocation/removal creates the subject's authenticated in-app notice in account-subject state independently of the membership being ended. An additional external lifecycle copy may use only the subject's verified private safety channel under `RI-93-012`; missing, unavailable, stale, compromised, or failed safety delivery leaves the notice in-app-only and never falls back to an ordinary destination.
9. **Personal-state privacy and membership scope.** No message reveals another person's destinations, preferences, alert state, block list, or ceremony details. A ceremony-nominated notification destination is bound to the accepted membership and budget space; ending that membership ends its use without affecting a separately authorized use of the same destination in another space.
10. **Authoritative outcomes.** Client timeout, provider callback, worker delay, retry, or duplicate request never creates a second outcome. Copy reports the authoritative record after reconciliation and never reports partial success.
11. **Terminology.** Role names and lifecycle terms use the approved CBD-72 vocabulary exactly.

## 3. Open issues controlling this draft

> [!WARNING]
> **OPEN ISSUE `OI-73-001` — wrong-recipient and misdirected-invitation policy.** The safe disclosure ceiling, authenticated acceptance evidence, ordinary removal controls, and non-reversal rule are specified, but the Product Owner/source decision must settle whether a person who controls a mistakenly targeted channel may accept and whether any additional corrective flow exists. No copy may imply that channel verification proves the inviter selected the intended human.

> [!WARNING]
> **OPEN ISSUE `OI-73-002` — alternative no-account decline UX.** The safe interim rule is settled: after exact invited-channel verification, a holder without an authenticated account may decline this invitation once; a persistent decline-and-block action remains unavailable until an authenticated account is attached; and ambiguity, abandonment, or interruption creates neither. Protected disclosure, destination nomination, consent, and acceptance also remain authenticated-account-only. The Product Owner/source decision is open only on whether a different no-account UX should replace this interim behavior.

> [!WARNING]
> **OPEN ISSUE `OI-73-003` — governing CBD-91 data classes and focused source alignment.** The final data-class assignments for synthetic suppression state, ceremony/account/destination proofs, membership-scoped destinations, outcome reconciliation, and subject lifecycle notices remain unresolved, as does the focused CBD-72/CBD-91 source alignment required by `RI-93-012` to replace older every-channel lifecycle routing. This does not reopen CBD-73's normative rule: lifecycle notice is mandatory in-app, may additionally use only a verified private safety channel, has no ordinary-channel fallback, and remains in-app-only when that safety channel is unavailable. Copy must not make retention, deletion, or custody promises beyond the current approved class rules.

> [!WARNING]
> **OPEN ISSUE `OI-73-004` — exact copy evidence and decline/expiry presentation.** All rows below are semantic contracts only. Exact strings, the safe non-attributing decline/expiry presentation, channel templates, localization equivalence, accessibility/comprehension evidence, and specialist privacy/safety review remain open.

> [!WARNING]
> **OPEN ISSUE `OI-73-008` — concrete code, verification, and rate design.** Message semantics require opaque codes, bounded uniform verification, and non-enumerating rate outcomes, but exact entropy, proof mechanism, expiry duration, attempt values, and resource/rate parameters remain architecture evidence.

> [!WARNING]
> **OPEN ISSUE `OI-73-009` — invalidation mechanism evidence.** The copy assumes immediate session/cache/job/queue/package/destination cutoff and authoritative reconciliation; exact mechanisms and measured evidence remain open and copy may not promise stronger timing than the evidence supports.

> [!WARNING]
> **OPEN ISSUE `OI-73-010` — inviter-block and pair-limiter engineering.** Account-bound block matching, alias/token handling, privacy-preserving recipient and inviter key derivation, separation of no-account limiter evaluation from account-only block evaluation, synthetic-record storage, limiter values, recovery, and response-padding implementation remain open even though presentation equivalence and account-existence non-enumeration are normative.

> [!WARNING]
> **OPEN ISSUE `OI-73-011` — audit storage.** Audience projections and prohibited disclosure are specified, while integrity, retention, storage, and operational-access design remain open.

> [!WARNING]
> **OPEN ISSUE `OI-73-012` — account creation and sign-in mechanics.** Canonical ceremony ordering and authority boundaries are normative; identity-provider, new-account, session-assurance, and return-to-ceremony mechanics remain open.

## 5. Coverage rule

Every lifecycle transition, checklist item, proposal terminal outcome, transfer terminal outcome, and reconciliation state that names customer copy resolves to exactly one semantic row above. Rows reused for security equivalence are intentionally shared: ordinary and synthetic invitation requests use `MSG-73-001` and `MSG-73-006`; every normalized unaccepted terminal projection and every actually unusable-code class uses `MSG-73-003`. Restricted provider-failure metadata is not an unusable-code class and emits no customer message. `MSG-73-020` is a deprecated non-emitting compatibility alias to `MSG-73-003`, never a distinct normative edge. Ordinary-member and Co-owner removal precommit confirmations remain distinct as `MSG-73-047` and `MSG-73-049`. A new row must never create an observable distinction that the non-enumeration contract forbids.

Personal-control and recovery edges are explicit: TR-73-29 uses recipient-only `MSG-73-024`; TR-73-41 uses conditional non-authorizing `MSG-73-025`; TR-73-34 uses membership-end denial/no-op `MSG-73-026`; mutating TR-73-46 invalidation uses both-party `MSG-73-027`, while mutation-free TR-73-47 denial/no-op exclusively uses `MSG-73-046`; and TR-73-25, TR-73-27, and TR-73-28 use `MSG-73-029` without implying a mixed command was split.

This v0.2 inventory contains **48 stable message rows**. The compatibility alias is counted as a reserved stable ID but never as a separate customer-visible message or template.
